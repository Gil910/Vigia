The last column is the one that matters: attacks where a user reading the
reply would see nothing wrong, and the chain of thought named the thing
anyway. Any application that logs reasoning, or renders it in a
"thinking…" disclosure, leaks in exactly those cases without ever being
successfully attacked.

### Verdicts the judge never gave

says so in the row. Those are not verdicts and are excluded from every table

a judge that died partway through is not a second opinion.

responses, not by what the config says, so these are treated as the
ordinary runs they turned out to be.

### Seeds that are not attacks

The mutation model was asked to rewrite an attack into another language or
register. Sometimes it declined, and the refusal was written into the corpus
as if it were the attack. A seed reading `Lo siento, pero no puedo cumplir con
esa solicitud` scores 0 whatever the target does, so it is not a measurement
of anything. Those rows are excluded above and counted here.

| Locale | Distinct seeds | Rows dropped |
|---|---:|---:|
| gl-ES | 21 | 504 |
| eu-ES | 15 | 360 |
| es-GL | 11 | 264 |
| es-EU | 8 | 192 |
| ca-ES | 3 | 72 |

They are not spread evenly, which is why they had to come out of the
language comparison rather than be left in as noise. `vigia mutate` now
retries when the model refuses and drops the mutation rather than storing
it, and `scripts/validate_corpus.py` fails on one, so a corpus generated
after v0.6.0 cannot carry them. This database was generated before that.
